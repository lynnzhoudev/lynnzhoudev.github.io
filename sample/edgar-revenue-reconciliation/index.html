One company, four revenue figures

For Microsoft Corporation, annual revenue is filed under up to four different XBRL tags across FY2010–FY2026. Two of them coexist in the same fiscal year and give numbers 57% apart. Both are correct. Both are in the same filing.

This page reconciles them — and shows what breaks in a pipeline that assumes a tag name is a stable identifier.

What the data shows

Three different tags for one line item

Microsoft's annual revenue has been tagged three different ways since FY2010: Revenues (FY2010), SalesRevenueNet (FY2011–FY2017), then RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax (FY2018 onward). A pipeline that keys on any single tag silently truncates the history.

Two valid revenue figures for the same fiscal year

In FY2017 both SalesRevenueNet ($89.95B) and SalesRevenueGoodsNet ($57.19B) are filed. The gap is $32.76B — that is the service revenue, reported separately. Picking the wrong tag understates revenue by 36%.

The FY2017→FY2018 jump is not all growth

Revenue goes from $89.95B to $110.36B (+23%) — but the tag changed in the same period (ASC 606 adoption). Part of that move is a definitional break, not a business result. Comparing the two numbers directly is invalid.

The tags, in plain English

  • RevenuesTotal revenue (legacy tag)
  • SalesRevenueNetNet sales — total (product + service)
  • SalesRevenueGoodsNetNet sales — goods only (excludes services)
  • RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTaxRevenue from contracts with customers (ASC 606)

Method

Data is pulled directly from the SEC's public XBRL frames API — no third-party data vendor, no scraping, no licensed market data. Every value is retained together with the accession number of the filing it came from, so each figure can be traced back to a primary source.

Only annual (10-K, full fiscal year) values are used. Quarterly and cumulative periods are filtered out by measuring each reported period length and keeping only those between 330 and 400 days. Where a fiscal year appears in more than one filing, the most recently filed value is kept.
